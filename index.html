Suggested changes

12 changes, ranked by impact against effort. Each line shows the suggestion; open it for the data behind it. None of them changes what already works, such as the trade clips and the creator network.

For the main Tradeify page

1A link in the bioOne landing page in the bio for the current campaign.Quick fix▶
The bio currently links only to Threads, and seven captions point viewers to the bio. Lucid's bio has links to its website, store, YouTube and Facebook.
Suggestion: one landing page in the bio with the current campaign at the top, followed by the evaluation page, PTL and 247/FX. It would be updated whenever a campaign changes.
2Posts leading up to each launchA four-post sequence: teaser, hint, launch, then a creator using it.High▶
Lucid's Prop Pass was teased before launch and got 380k plays across seven posts. Tradeify's Rewards Center got 96k across four. Lucid's second teaser alone (55.2k) got more plays than any Rewards Center post. On @tradeify.fx, the waitlist Reel posted before launch is still the best-performing post.
Suggestion: a four-post sequence for each launch: a teaser three days before, a closer hint the day before, the launch post, and a creator using the product two days after.
3One code and one end date on every channelA shared offer sheet that social, email, paid and affiliates all use.High▶
Lucid's VAULT code appeared with its end date on screen in every post, so every channel showed the same offer.
Suggestion: a shared offer sheet listing the code, discount, start and end date, and approved creative for each live offer. Social, email, paid and affiliates would all use it as their reference.
4Showing what is inside the packsReels of creators opening packs and showing what they won.High▶
The Rewards Center captions explain how to earn packs: missions, Gems, packs. The cover art shows the prizes (a 150K account, a Rolex), but no Reel shows someone opening a pack and getting one.
Suggestion: Reels of creators opening packs and reacting to what they get, a weekly "best pull" Reel from traders' own screen recordings, and a running count of how many 150K accounts have been won.
5A consistent voice for launch postsOne launch post written in Tradeify's own humorous tone, as a test.Worth testing▶
Lucid writes its product posts as jokes, and they get more plays than its median. Tradeify already uses humour in its memes ("This is not financial advice. This is peer pressure." got 24.9k), but not in its launch posts.
Suggestion: write one launch post in Tradeify's own humorous tone and compare its results with a standard announcement.

For Tradeify 247 and Tradeify FX

1Using the main page's audience247 and FX launches posted as collabs with @tradeifyfunding.Highest▶
@tradeify.fx has 2,845 followers and @tradeifyfunding has 93.1k. Across the 51 main-page Reels, FX's launch week had no Reel, and 247 had one (23 September, 16.2k plays).
Suggestion: post 247 and FX launches as Instagram collab posts with @tradeifyfunding, so one Reel appears on both pages. The main page already does this with its creators.
2Proof on the grid, discounts in StoriesPayouts and traders on the grid; codes and countdowns in Stories.High▶
On @tradeify.fx, the payout certificate got 582 likes and the waitlist Reel 684. The two discount-only graphics got 55 and 46, the lowest on the page. Traders comparing firms want to see that a firm pays, and the Trustpilot reviews reflect the same.
Suggestion: use the grid and Reels for payouts, traders and the FX Open, and use Stories and the link page for codes and countdowns.
3A series based on 247's trading hoursA weekly "weekend desk" series for 247, and a session-open series for FX.High▶
The main difference between 247 and futures is time: futures traders stop on Friday, 247 traders can keep trading over the weekend.
Suggestion: a weekly "weekend desk" series showing 247 traders trading BTC or tokenized stocks on a Saturday, in the same format each week. For FX, a series built around the Tokyo, London and New York session opens, filmed by creators in those time zones.
4The "W or L" format for crypto, gold and forexThe main page's best format, recorded on MT5 and DXTrade.High▶
The main page's best repeatable format (29.4k median) is not used on @tradeify.fx, which has two Reels in total. It needs only a screen recording and a person on camera.
Suggestion: the same format recorded on MT5 and DXTrade, so viewers see the platform they would trade on. For FX, creators whose audiences are outside the US, since FX is not sold in the US.
5Daily coverage of the FX OpenA daily leaderboard Reel featuring one trader's story.Medium▶
Registration closed on 11 October. Lucid's competition posts got 42.6k and 47.1k plays on days one and two, with each day showing a leader and a result.
Suggestion: a daily leaderboard Reel featuring one trader's story, and a winners' post presented as a payout rather than a list.
6Handles, links and the pricing pageOne reference sheet for handles, links and live codes.Quick fix▶
These are the inconsistencies I found while researching:
  • The 247 site's footer links Instagram to @tradeify247, which does not exist; its header links to @tradeify.fx.
  • On the same footer, the X icon opens Discord and the TikTok icon opens X.
  • X is split across @tradeify247 and @tradeifyforex, while YouTube is one @TradeifyFXOfficial channel.
  • The FX pricing section shows placeholder text ("Lorem ipsum", "Save $X with code DASH") next to the live OCT code.
Suggestion: one sheet of handles, links and live codes that every channel is checked against.
7Rewards on 247 or FX, if they launchPacks opened on camera, showing what was won.Later▶
Crypto traders already understand rarity, drops and collections, so the pack lesson from Lucid applies here too.
Suggestion: show packs being opened on camera, with what was won: a funded account, a discount or a rare item.

B

Tradeify's main page

What works

  • Creators share to Tradeify's page@amas.pft, @_claudiarea, @ZaeemTrades and others post from their own profiles and share to Tradeify's. Each trade clip reaches both audiences, which is how 51 Reels added up to 1.87M plays in a month.
  • The "W or L trade" formatTen Reels with the same caption, a median of 29.4k plays, and highs of 59.4k and 58.5k. Each is a screen recording, a person and a result. They are cheap to make and get more plays than most produced content on the page.
  • Strategy breakdowns by the same creators"My EXACT trading strategy explained" got 65.4k and the iFVG breakdown 32.7k.
  • Athlete partnershipsAdesanya (252k), Littler (200k) and Head (140k) in one month. Lucid has nothing similar.
  • A series with a followingDoorDash to Payout with @ZaeemTrades got 119, 166 and 172 comments on 12k to 19k plays, about ten times the page's usual comment rate.
  • Posting volumeAbout 1.6 Reels a day, with consistent quality.

What could be improved

  • "Link in bio" with no linkThe PTL sign-up, Profit Wars and FUNDED posts point to the bio, which only links to Threads. Lucid's bio has four links. These posts had about 150k plays.
  • Launches are announced in one postThe Rewards Center launched on 11 September in one post (24.1k) with no posts before it. Its four posts averaged 24k. Lucid's equivalent had seven posts and a 163k high. The FX page did post before its launch.
  • FUNDED podcast clips get fewer playsSix clips, a median of 10.8k and a 0.45% like rate, against 23k and 1.33% for the page. The guests are strong, but the opening seconds do not say what the viewer will learn.
C

Lucid Trading

Fewer posts, with a very consistent style. Most posts are written as jokes about the company itself.

Strengths

  • More plays per postA median of 44.8k plays across 30 Reels, with no collabs.
  • Launches are spread over several postsThe Prop Pass ran "SOON" (29.4k), "you're sweating" (55.2k), the launch (163k), then four follow-ups. The $2M competition opened with "How to win TBD" (61k) and a joke terms-and-conditions post (95.3k).
  • A consistent voiceFor example, "The $2 Million Dollar Trading Competition Based On Nothing In Particular Event". The rock-marketplace post got 2,974 comments, most of them continuing the joke.
  • One code and one end date"40% off, code VAULT" with the end date on screen, posted on 21 August and 14 September (57k and 64.9k).
  • Product announcements written as jokesOptional DLL (66.8k), the mobile app (83.7k) and live cash-out (74.8k) were all above the median.

Weaknesses

  • Many live-stream posters8 of 30 Reels promote the Sunday YouTube show. They are the weakest group, with a 37.5k median and a 0.53% like rate.
  • Almost no trading contentNo trade clips and one strategy-related post out of 30.
  • Inside jokes can confuse new viewersRunning characters such as AJ, Leo, Santo and Minh work for regular viewers. The competition wrap-up got only 14.3k.
  • Low outputAbout three Reels a week.

Similarities

  • Both are futures prop firms selling similar products: daily-payout accounts, optional daily loss limits, fast funding.
  • Both launched pack-opening rewards within 16 days of each other.
  • Both ran a seven-figure trading competition in September.
  • They share a creator: @raketrades posted PTL Day 1 for Tradeify and hosted Lucid's 5 October live stream.
  • Both use dark designs with green accents and show P&L on screen. Neither uses Story highlights.

Differences

  • ReachTradeify's reach comes from its creators; Lucid's comes from its brand voice.
  • Marketing approachTradeify promotes the product and the offer; Lucid promotes its people and its humour (see E).
  • Volume and content51 Reels against 15 in the same month. Tradeify shows trading; Lucid shows the company.
  • Launches and offersTradeify announces launches in one post; Lucid posts before them. Lucid repeats one code and date; Tradeify runs several campaigns at once.
  • Off-platformLucid sends viewers to YouTube every week and links it in its bio; Tradeify's FUNDED clips point to a link that is not there.
E

Two marketing approaches

Lucid markets in a similar way to Red Bull: it promotes its people, its running jokes and its collectibles, and the product is part of that. Tradeify promotes the product directly: what it is, how it works, and the offer. The pack launches show the difference most clearly.

How Lucid does it

  • Staff appear as recurring characters, so viewers follow the people as well as the firm.
  • Events are given joke names.
  • Rewards can be collected and traded on a marketplace after the pack is opened.
  • A weekly show at a fixed time, run like a channel.
  • Product news is usually presented as a joke. Optional DLL, the app and live cash-out all got more plays than the median.

How Tradeify does it

  • Captions list features: "Trade. Rip packs. Claim rewards. Repeat."
  • Several offers, deadlines and "link in bio" posts run at the same time.
  • The bio lists specifications: $750k, daily payouts, $250M+ paid out.
Tradeify already does this in some posts. The W or L clips, DoorDash to Payout, the PTL Creator League and the athlete giveaways are about people rather than offers, and they get more plays and comments than the product posts.

Limits of this comparisonProduct-focused marketing is what drives sign-ups before a deadline, and sign-ups are not visible from outside. Lucid's approach needs a steady voice and steady output, and its live-stream posters (its weakest group) show what happens when posts are about the brand but not entertaining. For Tradeify, the opportunity is to present more product launches the way it already presents its creator content.
